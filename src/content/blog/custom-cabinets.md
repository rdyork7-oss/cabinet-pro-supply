“Custom” gets used for everything from a local shop build to a fully modified factory line. The word doesn’t guarantee quality. What does: proven box engineering (especially corners), a durable factory finish, and a manufacturer that has already solved the hardware problems you’re about to invent.

## Why established custom factories win

At Cabinet Pro Supply we partner with established custom and semi-custom manufacturers. Every cabinet in the spec book is a proven design with the right hardware. True custom programs go further: modifications are available on virtually everything—door and drawer edges, center panel materials, rail size, finish sheen, levels of distressing, and more.

People at large custom factories know cabinetry deeply—not because a local craftsman cannot be skilled, but because size brings resources, national (and international) experience, and a huge database of trial and error to learn from.

One of the biggest reasons to buy a factory-made cabinet is the finishing process. Large manufacturers share common procedures such as heat-curing finishes. Whether you are talking high-end custom or entry-level stock, that goes a long way toward a finish that lasts a lifetime. Compare it to factory paint on a new car versus a DIY repaint: both can look nice, but there is no substitute for the beauty and durability of a proper factory finish.

## Custom, semi-custom, and value

Full custom isn’t always the right spend. Many of the brands we carry offer scaled custom and semi-custom programs—same finishing discipline and warranties, fewer SKUs—so you can put budget into the look and layout that matter for the whole kitchen. We also carry CabFabs stock when speed and a simple painted shaker package fit the job; we won’t dress stock up as made-to-order American custom.

All things being equal, custom cabinets are better—and so are plywood boxes. But all things are not equal, and you typically get what you pay for depending on where you purchase. A name-brand stock or semi-custom line from a manufacturer that stands behind a lifetime warranty will often look better the day you install it and hold up better over time than a generic “custom” label with no finishing discipline behind it.
