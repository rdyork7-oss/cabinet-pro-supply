Inset doors sit inside the face frame so the door face is flush with the frame—magazine kitchens and furniture-like detail. That look depends on tight manufacturing tolerances and careful install, which is why inset usually lives in higher-end custom and semi-custom programs—not commodity stock.

With decorative hardware and refrigerator latches, you can even get a modern retro aesthetic that gives a kitchen project a unique, finished presence.

## How inset works

Inset cabinetry refers to face-frame cabinets with the door set inside the face frame so it sits flush with the front of the cabinet—instead of overlaying the face frame or cabinet box, as is typical for other cabinet types. This is achieved with special hinges (hidden or decorative) and by meticulously fitting the door inside the frame opening. Proper fit is critical to keep doors from sticking or showing uneven gaps.

Because of how precise inset construction must be, it is not typically offered by lower-priced manufacturers or importers. We offer inset from our custom-capable manufacturers—name-brand makers on the higher end of kitchen cabinetry.

## Installation matters

Inset rewards installers who have done it before. Cabinets must be perfectly level. If they are hung crooked or out of square, doors may not close properly, or gaps between door and face frame can look larger than intended.

Ask how many inset kitchens they’ve hung and how they handle adjustments. If you’re a DIY remodeler, plan for professional install on inset—or consider full overlay for a cleaner DIY path ([For DIY](/diy)). Look for installers who are experienced, comfortable with inset, and unfazed when asked about it.
