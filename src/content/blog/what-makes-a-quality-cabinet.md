What makes a great quality cabinet may seem subjective, but in practice it is often clearer than people expect. Over the years we have worked with many cabinet brands. Even when they all have nice displays and samples, there is a huge difference in overall customer satisfaction between brands and product types.

When a potential client contacts us for the first time, we ask about the project, timeframe, and style they are looking for so we can recommend products that fit the budget. The more satisfied they are with the product, the more likely they are to come back the next time they need cabinets.

## Spec sheets can mislead

So what actually makes a high-quality cabinet? Several factors go into it. Spec sheets can be misleading. Many lesser lines list features that sound strong—soft-close doors and drawers, plywood boxes, and so on. Those features are nice, but they do not always equal a high-quality cabinet.

Soft-close and “plywood box” show up on good and mediocre lines. Ask next: door material and grade, how the finish is cured, drawer construction, and who warranties the product—and whether that brand is still around to honor it. What is often missing from the sheet is what the face of the cabinet is made of and how it is finished.

What the door and frame are made of is far more important to overall quality than whether the box is plywood or engineered wood—both are engineered products if you think about it. A trip to the home center lumber aisle shows how many grades of plywood exist. The same is true of dovetail drawers: some use clear aspen or maple and finish every joint smoothly; others use lower-grade woods and leave joints rough.

## Doors, finish, and quality control

For stained wood, higher-grade maple, cherry, alder, walnut, and oak still matter. For painted cabinets, stable paint-grade materials (often MDF centers with hardwood or paint-grade frames) usually outperform solid maple that moves and telegraphs joint lines—see our notes on [painted cabinets](/blog/painted-cabinets).

A high-quality cabinet is then sanded well and finished by trained professionals with serious factory equipment, where the finish can be cured or baked into a surface that stands the test of time.

Last but not least, quality control matters enormously—and it never shows up on a spec sheet. Conscientious workers make a huge difference. Experienced kitchen designers know the quality differences between lines, and when you visit a showroom they will typically walk you through good, better, and best.

## Before you buy, ask

- Lifetime (or clear limited) warranty from a name brand?
- Factory-cured finish, not field paint?
- Drawers: hardwood dovetail + soft-close guides?
- Will the dealer help with a complete kitchen takeoff—not just sell boxes?
